# Plain-English Guide

## What Problem Does ReconForge Solve?

ReconForge ERP helps teams check whether inventory, accounting, work orders, purchases, invoices, and WIP agree with each other. It turns ERP exports into exception reports that finance, stores, workshops, and auditors can review.

## What Is Stock vs GL Reconciliation?

Stock movements show what happened in stores or inventory: parts issued, received, adjusted, or fitted. GL entries show what was posted in accounting. Stock vs GL reconciliation checks that the operational movement and accounting posting agree.

## What Is WIP Aging?

WIP means work in progress. WIP aging shows work orders that have remained open for too long. Old WIP can indicate jobs not closed, costs not invoiced, parts not returned, or accounting not finalized.

## What Is a Work Order?

A work order is the job record that links customer or equipment work to parts, labor, costs, invoices, and closure. In workshops, fleet maintenance, dealerships, and service centers, work orders are the bridge between operations and finance.

## What Is an Exception?

An exception is something that needs review. Examples include a stock issue with no GL entry, a GL cost with no stock movement, a missing work order, a direct purchase fitted without store receipt, or an old part not returned.

## What Is a Risk Score?

ReconForge assigns each exception a score from 0 to 100. Low scores are routine review items. High and Critical scores indicate issues that may affect financial accuracy, audit evidence, stock control, or customer billing.

## Why Direct Purchase-and-Fit Is Dangerous

Direct purchase-and-fit happens when a part is bought and installed without normal store receipt and issue. This can create audit, payment, and inventory risks because the part may bypass stock controls and be harder to match to the work order and GL.

## Why Old-Part Returns Matter

When a new spare part is issued, some companies require the old damaged part to be returned. Missing old-part returns can indicate weak control, warranty risk, unauthorized consumption, or incomplete job evidence.

## How Finance Uses the Report

Finance reviews unmatched GL entries, unmatched stock movements, value differences, WIP aging, missing invoices, and recommended actions before month-end close.

## How Stores Uses the Report

Stores teams review missing source documents, invalid product codes, direct-fit movements, cancelled purchase order links, and old-part return gaps.

## How Workshop Managers Use the Report

Workshop managers review work orders with parts but no invoice, costs after closure, stale WIP, repeated repair patterns, and high-risk jobs.

## How Auditors Use the Evidence Binder

Auditors can open each High or Critical exception folder and review the summary, source records, match candidates, triggered rules, recommended action, and audit trail.
